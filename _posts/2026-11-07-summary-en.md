rss · Solidot · Nov 6, 17:20

**「Peer Production Model and Open Source Ecosystem」** The peer production model, exemplified by Linux and Wikipedia, represents an alternative to traditional economic models. It challenges the notion that private, for-profit enterprises are the most effective way to facilitate collaboration and allocate the outcomes of such collaboration. In the peer production model, participants contribute labor without contractual obligations or economic incentives, and the resulting products are considered public resources, protected by non-excludable intellectual property rights. This model, proposed by legal scholar Yochai Benkler, offers a different approach to coordination, organization, and resource allocation in collaborative projects.

**「Impact」** The analysis suggests that the peer production model challenges traditional economic assumptions by providing an alternative for collaboration and resource allocation without relying on private, for-profit enterprises, potentially leading to new approaches in software engineering and open source development.
