rss · Solidot · Nov 6, 17:20

**「Peer Production Model and Its Implications」** The peer production model, introduced by legal scholar Yochai Benkler, represents a mode of socio-economic production where a large number of participants collaborate through the internet. This model contrasts with traditional economic theories that rely on private for-profit enterprises for cooperation and efficient distribution of outcomes. In peer production, there is no reliance on contractual obligations, economic incentives, or bureaucratic authority to ensure participation or effective organization of labor. The resulting products are public resources, protected by non-excludable intellectual property rights, making direct pricing in the open market impossible. Ideal peer production projects maintain complete collective autonomy over the scope of work, the legitimacy of leadership authority, and the selection of tasks worth investing in. However, in practice, peer production is often applied in varying degrees and by entities that may not fully meet all the defining criteria.

**「Impact」** This analysis provides developers and organizations with a deeper understanding of the peer production model, which can inform their strategies for open source projects and collaboration, potentially leading to more effective and sustainable open source initiatives.
