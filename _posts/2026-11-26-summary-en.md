rss · Solidot · Nov 25, 14:18

**「Background on Supercomputer Maintenance」** Supercomputers, such as the Frontier at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, consist of thousands of computing nodes, making their maintenance a significant challenge. Issues such as uncorrectable memory errors and maintenance mistakes can lead to problems. Technicians working in cramped racks may accidentally restart incorrect nodes or accidentally touch the power of adjacent nodes. Hardware failures can affect individual nodes, but for simulations running on thousands of processors, this can lead to the entire process gradually stopping. The Frontier supercomputer has a probability of 1.5 occurrences per day of uncorrectable double-bit errors in its HBM memory, compared to the previous generation supercomputer Summit, which only had such errors once every two weeks. This is due to the Frontier&\#x27;s memory capacity being over 16 times that of Summit. In addition to memory, problematic GPU firmware and interconnect faults can also cause serious issues. A single GPU failure can force a computation task using 1,024 GPUs to stop. GPUs with performance issues also pose problems: if a computing GPU unit runs at only 80% of normal speed, the other units may need to stop waiting for it. Supercomputers use checkpointing mechanisms to save computation results at regular intervals, so that simulation tasks do not need to start from scratch in case of an error.

**「Impact on High-Performance Computing Maintenance」** The maintenance of supercomputers like the Frontier at Oak Ridge National Laboratory presents significant challenges due to hardware failures such as memory errors and GPU issues. These failures can disrupt large-scale simulations, necessitating the need to determine the last stored computation progress and the amount of work that needs to be redone. The increased frequency of memory errors in Frontier compared to its predecessor, Summit, underscores the importance of robust maintenance and checkpointing mechanisms to ensure the continuity of complex simulations.
